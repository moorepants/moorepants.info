======================================
Human-in-the-Loop AI, Is it Necessary?
======================================

:authors: Jason K. Moore
:date: 2026-11-24 07:39:00
:description: Engineers may need to know the same things they always have in
              the age of AI.
:category: education
:tags: generative ai, education, tu delft

The chair of the TU Delft executive board, Ingrid Thijssen, implied in a recent
interview_ that we may have to "completely overhaul our curriculum" given the
current and upcoming state of generative AI. University educators around the
world are grappling with such statements and ideas. This statement implies that
maybe students need to know different things or have different skills in a
post-AI world. I believe the central question to first address is whether
humans belong in the AI engineering loop. What our curriculum will be depends
on that answer.

.. _interview: https://delta.tudelft.nl/en/article/executive-board-chair-if-ai-transfers-knowledge-and-conducts-research-what-is-the-purpose-of-a-university

It is now possible to see a near-future where self-driven cars cause fewer
crashes, injuries, and deaths than human-driven cars do. So humans may not be
trained in the knowledge and skills for driving in the future. Will this also
be the case for human-executed engineering?

It has always been the case that humans review engineering work to vet whether
new things we create meet the needs, specifications, and standards for solving
the problem at hand. We apply our learned engineering knowledge to do that
vetting. Generative AI can now do engineering (with coaching) and possibly, in
some cases, even to the degree that the outcomes are thoroughly engineered with
no flaws that human engineers might catch that AI didn't. So, should a human
have to sign off on AI engineering? Must an engineer thoroughly check that the
outcome is correct, sufficient, sound? If the answer to this will soon be "no",
then we apparently no longer need human engineers. But even if the answer could
be "no", should it be "no"?

If we assume that the answer cannot or should not be "no", then we can return
to the curriculum statement and to what engineers should learn.  If a human
must, at least, check the engineering work done by generative AI, shouldn't
they know mostly the same things about engineering that society has always
expected them to know? For a human to evaluate others' engineering work,
whether AI generated or not, we send them through years of intensive training
that builds the set of skills and knowledge necessary to be an engineer. If we
should not remove the human from the AI engineering loop, then I do not think
our engineering curriculum should change all that much. I conjecture that an
engineer who must do engineering themselves needs the same knowledge and mostly
the same skills to check the engineering of others, including that of
generative AI.

If Ingrid Thijssen's comment is implying that she believes human's should not
be in the AI engineering loop, then our curriculum may serve no purpose at all;
a university may serve no purpose at all. It is hard to see a future with
humans not embedded firmly in the engineering loop other than one that leads
towards a general state of idiocracy_.

.. _idiocracy: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiocracy
