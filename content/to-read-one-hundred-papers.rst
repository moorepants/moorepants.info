You Don't Expect Me to Read 100 Papers, Do You?
===============================================

:authors: Jason K. Moore
:date: 2026-09-16 18:30:00
:description: A student questioned why would you read 100 academic papers.
:category: education
:tags: student, generative ai, tu delft

I was the (co-)grader today for presentations about MSc students' literature
studies. A student presenter shared that they had read through 100 papers to
find the results for their literature study. Following the presentation, a
student in the audience asked something like: "One hundred papers sounds like a
lot, did you really read that many papers? Why didn't you use AI to go through
them?".

Firstly, it was interesting that the audience member thought that reading 100
papers was unfathomable. The number may seem large, but assessing 100 papers
for a literature study is not an extreme, more the norm. Whether you read 100
word-for-word for a 20-40 page literature study may not be the norm, but
manually reviewing that many is. I can't say that I've explicitly heard the
concern before, although students do often ask how many papers are sufficient
for a literature study. [1]_

Secondly, I am not sure the audience realized this but the speaker had a
strong command of the material in their study and all other questions were
easily and fully answered. The ability to do this is the product of reading 100
papers on a topic. The relevant information in the papers was inserted into the
speakers brain through the action of reading and writing themselves.

Each day, more things point towards the negative effect generative AI is having
on learning. I guess we will have to persuade new students to actually read the
papers from now on, when in the past that was a given.

.. [1] I do not give a numerical answer to this question, btw.
